# ROI & Risk Assessment — AI E-commerce Conversion & Shopping Advisor

## 1. Objective

This document assesses the expected financial value and principal risks associated with the **AI E-commerce Conversion & Shopping Advisor**.

The solution analyses permitted customer-journey signals and recommends an appropriate next action, such as:

* on-site AI shopping assistance;
* product comparison;
* recovery of an abandoned cart or checkout;
* CRM follow-up;
* permitted customer communication;
* no action where intervention is not appropriate.

The POC demonstrates technical feasibility but does **not** establish production-level ROI or conversion uplift.

All financial figures in this document are preliminary assumptions and must be validated during a controlled pilot using real business data.

---

## 2. Cost Assumptions

### Upfront Implementation

Estimated initial implementation cost: **€4,500**

This estimate includes:

* business analysis and use-case design;
* n8n workflow implementation;
* AI prompt and decision-logic design;
* testing and evaluation;
* monitoring setup;
* documentation.

### Ongoing Operating Cost

Estimated monthly operating cost: **€90–€400**

Potential operating costs include:

* automation infrastructure;
* LLM/API usage;
* monitoring and evaluation;
* email or messaging services;
* other production integrations.

For the base ROI scenario, an illustrative average operating cost of **€245/month** is used.

Estimated costs:

| Period                    | Estimated Cost |
| ------------------------- | -------------: |
| Initial implementation    |         €4,500 |
| Monthly operating cost    |           €245 |
| Year 1 operating cost     |         €2,940 |
| Year 1 total cost         |     **€7,440** |
| Three-year operating cost |         €8,820 |

These figures are estimates and should not be interpreted as confirmed production pricing.

## 3. Business Value Assumptions

The primary expected business value is generated by identifying visitors who demonstrate meaningful purchase intent and providing an appropriate intervention.

Potential value drivers include:

* reducing cart abandonment;
* reducing checkout abandonment;
* improving product-selection assistance;
* increasing conversion among high-intent visitors;
* improving customer experience;
* identifying recurring customer-journey problems.

The POC uses behavioural signals such as:

* product engagement;
* number of products viewed;
* time spent on relevant pages;
* returning visits;
* product questions;
* cart activity;
* checkout initiation;
* purchase status.

The POC does **not** establish an actual conversion uplift because no production A/B test has been conducted.

The pilot should therefore measure **incremental conversion and incremental revenue against a control group**.

---

## 4. Illustrative Business Scenario

For financial modelling only, assume that a production pilot eventually generates:

**€10,000 of incremental recovered revenue per month.**

Annual incremental value:

**€10,000 × 12 = €120,000**

This is an illustrative scenario and is **not a forecast, guarantee or demonstrated result**.

Actual business value will depend on:

* website traffic;
* baseline conversion rate;
* average order value;
* proportion of high-intent visitors;
* intervention effectiveness;
* customer acceptance;
* operating costs;
* legal and consent constraints.

---

## 5. Illustrative 12-Month ROI

Using the illustrative scenario:

* Incremental annual value: **€120,000**
* Year 1 total cost: **€7,440**
* Estimated net benefit: **€112,560**

ROI:

**(€120,000 − €7,440) / €7,440 × 100 ≈ 1,513%**

This result is highly sensitive to the assumed €10,000 monthly incremental revenue.

It should therefore be treated only as a scenario analysis.

The production business case should use measured incremental revenue from a controlled pilot rather than this assumption.

---

## 6. Illustrative 36-Month ROI

Assuming the same illustrative €10,000 monthly incremental value:

* Three-year incremental value: **€360,000**
* Three-year total cost: **€13,320**
* Estimated net benefit: **€346,680**

ROI:

**(€360,000 − €13,320) / €13,320 × 100 ≈ 2,603%**

Again, this is an illustrative scenario rather than a production forecast.

## 7. Break-Even Analysis

Using:

* Initial implementation: **€4,500**
* Monthly operating cost: **€245**

The first-month total cost is approximately:

**€4,500 + €245 = €4,745**

Under the illustrative €10,000 monthly incremental-value scenario, the initial investment would theoretically be recovered during the first month.

However, this should **not** be presented as the expected production payback period.

Actual break-even should be calculated using measured incremental contribution during the pilot.

The correct production calculation should consider:

**Incremental contribution − incremental operating costs − implementation costs**

## 8. ROI Validation Plan

The pilot should measure:

1. Number of visitors analysed.
2. Number of high-intent visitors detected.
3. Number of visitors receiving on-site assistance.
4. Number of recovery actions recommended.
5. Number of recovery actions activated.
6. Recovery conversion rate.
7. Incremental conversion versus control group.
8. Incremental recovered revenue.
9. Average order value.
10. Cost per AI interaction.
11. LLM token consumption.
12. AI latency.
13. False-positive rate.
14. Customer complaints.
15. Marketing opt-outs.
16. AI recommendation accuracy.

Where practical, a controlled A/B test should be used.

The final production ROI should be calculated using **incremental revenue**, rather than total revenue generated by customers exposed to the system.

## 10. Overall Risk Position

The POC is designed for e-commerce conversion optimisation and customer assistance.

It is not designed to make decisions concerning:

* employment;
* credit;
* insurance;
* healthcare;
* access to essential services;
* legal status;
* other high-impact domains.

The main production risks therefore relate to:

* GDPR and privacy;
* marketing consent;
* inappropriate profiling;
* AI reliability;
* hallucination;
* product-information accuracy;
* customer experience;
* integration reliability;
* AI operating costs;
* measurable commercial value.

These risks can be reduced through:

* privacy-by-design;
* data minimisation;
* consent and eligibility controls;
* catalogue grounding;
* structured AI outputs;
* validation rules;
* monitoring;
* human oversight where appropriate;
* controlled pilot testing.

The POC should **not** be presented as proof of production-level compliance, ROI or conversion uplift.

## 11. MVP Risk Controls

The current MVP includes or is designed to include several important controls:

### Privacy

* Anonymous visitors are not individually identified.
* Anonymous visitors are not contacted through identity-based outbound marketing.
* Sensitive personal characteristics are not used.
* Personal data sent to the LLM should be minimised.

### Marketing

* Marketing consent is explicitly represented.
* Marketing activation is separated from the AI recommendation.
* An email address alone does not authorise marketing communication.
* Consent/eligibility checks occur before outbound activation.

### AI reliability

* AI output is structured.
* The AI must rely on available behavioural evidence.
* The AI must not invent customer information.
* Product recommendations must be grounded in approved product information.
* Unknown information must remain unknown.

### Operational safety

* The AI primarily provides decision support.
* High-impact or unusual cases can be escalated to humans.
* AI/API failure should not prevent the underlying e-commerce experience from functioning.

## 12. Go / No-Go Criteria

Full production deployment should only be recommended if the pilot demonstrates:

### Business

* Positive incremental conversion versus the control group.
* Positive incremental revenue after relevant operating costs.
* A sustainable business case under conservative assumptions.

### AI Quality

* At least **80% correct classification** on the defined MVP validation scenarios.
* At least **80% appropriate next-best-action recommendations** on validation scenarios.
* At least **90% of product recommendations grounded in the available catalogue**.
* No material hallucination issues.

These thresholds apply to the project's validation dataset and should not be presented as production model-performance guarantees.

### Privacy and Compliance

* **100% of negative-marketing-consent test scenarios block outbound marketing activation.**
* No material privacy or consent violation occurs during the pilot.
* Applicable privacy and regulatory assessments are completed.

### Technical

* Stable workflow execution.
* Acceptable AI latency.
* Acceptable LLM cost.
* Working fallback mechanisms.
* Appropriate monitoring and logging.

### Customer Experience

* No material increase in complaints.
* Acceptable opt-out rate.
* Acceptable intervention frequency.
* Positive or neutral customer-experience indicators.

If these criteria are not met, the workflow should be optimised, the pilot extended, or deployment stopped before full rollout.

---

## 13. Final Recommendation

The recommended approach is **pilot-first deployment**.

The POC demonstrates that behavioural signals can be transformed into structured customer-journey insights and next-best-action recommendations.

However, the POC does not prove that the system will generate a specific revenue uplift or achieve production-level compliance.

The next step should therefore be a controlled pilot that measures:

**Behaviour → AI recommendation → Consent/eligibility → Intervention → Incremental conversion → Incremental revenue**

Only measured pilot results should be used to make the final production investment decision.
